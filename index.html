Investment Options

What You Can Invest In

Each metal behaves differently. Here is what each one is, why investors own it, and what to watch out for.

Au

Gold

The classic store of value

Gold has held its value for thousands of years. Most investors use it as the core of their metals holding, to protect wealth over the long term.

  • Benefit: Often holds up when stocks fall or inflation rises, and can be sold quickly anywhere in the world.
  • Risk: Pays no interest or dividends, and the price can fall or stay flat for years.
  • Ways to own: Bars (1 oz to 1 kg), coins, vaulted gold, or a gold IRA.
Ag

Silver

Affordable, with growth potential

Silver is both a precious metal and an industrial one, used in solar panels and electronics. Its lower price per ounce makes it an easy place to start.

  • Benefit: Low entry cost and growing industrial demand can lift prices.
  • Risk: Moves more sharply than gold in both directions, and takes up more vault space per dollar.
  • Ways to own: Bars (1 kg, 100 oz), coins, or vaulted silver.
Pt

Platinum

Rare, with industrial demand

Platinum is far rarer than gold and is widely used in cars, hydrogen technology, and jewellery. It suits investors who want exposure beyond gold and silver.

  • Benefit: Very limited supply, with prices that have historically been low relative to gold.
  • Risk: Prices follow the economy and car industry closely, and the market is smaller, so buy and sell spreads are wider.
  • Ways to own: 1 oz bars or vaulted platinum.
Pd

Palladium

A specialist, higher-risk metal

Palladium is mainly used in car exhaust systems. It is best kept as a small part of a wider metals portfolio.

  • Benefit: Adds variety, because its price often moves differently from gold.
  • Risk: The most volatile of the four. Supply comes from only a few countries.
  • Ways to own: 1 oz bars or vaulted palladium.

Managed Metals Portfolio

A ready-made mix

Not sure how to split your money? We build and rebalance a mix of all four metals for you, matched to how much risk you are comfortable with.

  • Benefit: Diversified across metals and vaults, managed by specialists.
  • Risk: Still tied to metal prices. A small management fee applies.
  • Ways to own: Fully allocated metal, held in your name.

Precious Metals IRA

Metals for retirement

Hold approved gold, silver, platinum, and palladium inside a tax-advantaged retirement account. You can open a new one or roll over an existing one.

  • Benefit: Possible tax advantages, plus long-term diversification for retirement savings.
  • Risk: IRS rules limit which metals qualify and when you can withdraw. Speak to a tax advisor first.
  • Ways to own: IRS-approved bars and coins held with a custodian.

Know Before You Invest

Benefits and Risks

Precious metals can strengthen a portfolio, but they are not right for every goal. Here is the full picture.

Why investors own metals

  • DiversificationMetals often move differently from stocks and bonds, which can smooth out the ups and downs of your overall portfolio.
  • Protection against inflationOver long periods, gold and silver have tended to keep their buying power when the value of paper money falls.
  • Real, tangible ownershipYou own specific bars and coins in your name. They are not a promise from a bank or company.
  • Easy to sellGold and silver are traded worldwide, every day. You can sell back to us on any business day.

What to consider

  • Prices go down as well as upMetal prices can fall sharply, and you may get back less than you put in.
  • No incomeUnlike shares or bonds, metals pay no dividends or interest. Returns come only from price changes.
  • CostsThere is a gap between buy and sell prices (the spread), plus a yearly storage and insurance fee. We show every cost before you confirm.
  • Best for the long termMetals work best as a long-term holding. Many investors keep them to a modest share of their portfolio, often 5–15%.

This information is educational and is not personal financial advice. Past performance does not guarantee future results. Please consider your own circumstances or speak with one of our advisors before investing.
