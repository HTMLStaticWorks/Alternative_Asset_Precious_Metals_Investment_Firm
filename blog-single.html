Why Central Banks Are Buying Gold at Record Pace

By James Hartwell • September 18, 2024 • 8 min read

In 2023, global central banks purchased a staggering 1,136 tonnes of gold — the second-highest annual total on record and a continuation of a trend that began accelerating in 2022. For precious metals investors, understanding why sovereign institutions are accumulating gold at this unprecedented pace is crucial for making informed allocation decisions.

The De-Dollarization Narrative

The most significant driver of central bank gold buying is a strategic shift away from US dollar-denominated reserves. The weaponization of the dollar through sanctions — most notably the freezing of Russian central bank assets in 2022 — sent a clear signal to sovereign wealth managers worldwide: dollar reserves carry political risk.

Countries like China, Turkey, Poland, and India have responded by significantly increasing their gold reserves as a neutral, un-sanctionable store of value. China alone added over 225 tonnes in 2023, bringing its official reserves to approximately 2,235 tonnes.

Gold is the only financial asset that is simultaneously nobody's liability. In an era of rising geopolitical risk, this property is more valuable than ever.

Inflation Protection at the Sovereign Level

Central banks experienced firsthand how rapidly inflation can erode the purchasing power of fiat currency reserves. With CPI reaching multi-decade highs across developed economies, gold's historical role as an inflation hedge has been revalidated at the institutional level.

Unlike bonds, gold cannot be debased by monetary policy. Unlike foreign currency reserves, it is not subject to the fiscal decisions of another sovereign. These properties make it an ideal component of a diversified reserve portfolio, particularly during periods of monetary uncertainty.

What This Means for Retail Investors

Central bank buying provides a structural floor for gold prices. When the largest institutional buyers in the world are consistently accumulating, it creates sustained demand that supports and elevates prices over time.

For individual investors, the message is clear: if the world's most sophisticated financial institutions are increasing their gold allocations, there is a compelling case for private investors to do the same. A well-constructed portfolio with 5–15% in physical precious metals offers the same benefits at the individual level that central banks are seeking at the sovereign level.
